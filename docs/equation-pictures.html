Phase portraits and curvature

The pictures for differential equations and differential geometry (roadmap 13.1 and 13.2): phase portraits with proved equilibria, solutions in time, the moving frame of a curve, and surfaces coloured by curvature.

Phase portraits

For a planar system x′=f(x,y)x' = f(x, y)x′=f(x,y), y′=g(x,y)y' = g(x, y)y′=g(x,y), phasePortrait([f, g], { x, y, orbits }) draws:

  • arrows of the direction field, all one length so only the direction shows;
  • the nullclines f=0f = 0f=0 (where motion is vertical) and g=0g = 0g=0 (horizontal), traced by marching squares;
  • orbits through the given points, followed forward and backward by Runge–Kutta in floats;
  • every equilibrium in the window, found and proved unique in a small box by analytic's planarSystem(...).equilibria, and labelled by its kind from the certified trace and determinant of the Jacobian: saddle when det⁡J<0\det J < 0detJ<0; a node or spiral, stable or not, by the sign of tr⁡J\operatorname{tr} JtrJ and of (tr⁡J)2−4det⁡J(\operatorname{tr} J)^2 - 4 \det J(trJ)2−4detJ.

The arrows and orbits are estimated; the equilibria are proved, and fig.equilibria has their boxes. When the search covers the whole window, complete says there are no others.

Curvature on a surface

curvatureSurface([x, y, z], { u, v }) colours each cell of a parametric surface by its Gaussian curvature K=(LN−M2)/(EG−F2)K = (LN - M^2)/(EG - F^2)K=(LN−M2)/(EG−F2), or by the mean curvature: red where it is positive (the surface bends the same way in every direction, like a sphere), blue where it is negative (a saddle), white near zero. On a torus the outside is red and the inside blue, and Gauss–Bonnet says the two cancel: ∬K dA=2πχ=0\iint K \, dA = 2\pi\chi = 0∬KdA=2πχ=0. curveOnSurface lifts a path in (u,v)(u, v)(u,v) to the surface, for a geodesic or the path of a transported vector.
