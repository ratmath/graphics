Pictures of numerical methods

The pictures for numerical analysis (roadmap 13.3): Newton's tangents, interpolation and Runge's phenomenon, the pieces of a quadrature rule, stability regions, and observed orders of convergence.

Newton's method

Newton's step replaces fff by its tangent at xkx_kxk​ and goes to where the tangent meets the axis: xk+1=xk−f(xk)/f′(xk)x_{k+1} = x_k - f(x_k)/f'(x_k)xk+1​=xk​−f(xk​)/f′(xk​). newtonFigure(f, x0, n) draws each tangent; near a simple root the error is squared at each step, so the tangents crowd together fast.

Interpolation

Through nnn points there is one polynomial of degree below nnn. interpolationFigure(f, n) builds it by divided differences and draws fff, the interpolant and the error. For f(x)=1/(1+25x2)f(x) = 1/(1 + 25x^2)f(x)=1/(1+25x2) on equally spaced points the error near the ends grows with nnn (Runge's phenomenon); with nodes: "chebyshev", the points cos⁡(2i+1)π2n\cos\frac{(2i+1)\pi}{2n}cos2n(2i+1)π​, it shrinks.

Quadrature

quadratureFigure(f, [a, b], n, { rule }) shades what the rule integrates: rectangles for the midpoint rule, trapezoids, or for Simpson's rule a parabola through each pair of panels, which integrates cubics exactly.
