Pictures for the Shelf

Pictures of numerical methods

The pictures for numerical analysis (roadmap 13.3): Newton's tangents, interpolation and Runge's phenomenon, the pieces of a quadrature rule, stability regions, and observed orders of convergence.

Newton's method

Newton's step replaces \(f\) by its tangent at \(x_k\) and goes to where the tangent meets the axis: \(x_{k+1} = x_k - f(x_k)/f'(x_k)\). newtonFigure(f, x0, n) draws each tangent; near a simple root the error is squared at each step, so the tangents crowd together fast.

Interpolation

Through \(n\) points there is one polynomial of degree below \(n\). interpolationFigure(f, n) builds it by divided differences and draws \(f\), the interpolant and the error. For \(f(x) = 1/(1 + 25x^2)\) on equally spaced points the error near the ends grows with \(n\) (Runge's phenomenon); with nodes: "chebyshev", the points \(\cos\frac{(2i+1)\pi}{2n}\), it shrinks.

Quadrature

quadratureFigure(f, [a, b], n, { rule }) shades what the rule integrates: rectangles for the midpoint rule, trapezoids, or for Simpson's rule a parabola through each pair of panels, which integrates cubics exactly.

Stability regions

A one-step method applied to \(y' = \lambda y\) multiplies \(y\) by \(R(h\lambda)\) at each step: \(R(z) = 1 + z\) for Euler, \(1 + z + z^2/2\) for Heun, \(\sum_{k \le 4} z^k/k!\) for the classical Runge–Kutta method. The solution stays bounded when \(|R(z)| \le 1\). stabilityRegion(R) shades that set; Euler's is the disc of radius 1 about \(-1\).

Orders of convergence

If the error behaves like \(C h^p\), then \(\log E = \log C + p \log h\): a line of slope \(p\) on log–log axes. convergenceFigure(rows) plots \((h, E)\) and fits the slope, the observed order.
